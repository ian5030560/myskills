# Choosing and filling in a standard checklist

Standard checklists judge how rigorous a study's design and conduct are. This is the only place the report judges design rigour; the content judgement does not repeat it.

## Choosing

Decide from reading the paper, not from its title or label. A study-design tag in a database (for example OpenAlex `study_designs`) is only a hint.

| What the paper is | Checklist |
|:---|:---|
| Randomised controlled trial | CASP RCT checklist |
| Cohort study | CASP cohort study checklist |
| Qualitative study | CASP qualitative checklist |
| Systematic review (with or without meta-analysis) of healthcare interventions | AMSTAR 2 |
| Systematic review on another question (for example an association, a risk factor or a non-clinical topic) | CASP systematic review checklist |
| Other empirical study: non-randomised intervention study, analytical cross-sectional study, case-control study, descriptive study (case series, survey, descriptive cross-sectional study), mixed methods study | MMAT |
| Not an empirical study (narrative review, theory, commentary, editorial), or empirical work no checklist here covers (for example method-and-benchmark papers, bibliometric or content analyses, economic evaluation, diagnostic accuracy) | None |

If two rows fit, use the more specific one and say why. A mixed methods study uses the MMAT on three categories as its file describes.

**When no checklist fits,** the report says in its external-assessment section that no standard checklist covers this design and gives the reason. Do not improvise questions and do not call this a weakness of the paper. Only the content judgement is done.

## Filling in

- Answer every question of the chosen checklist, in order, without skipping any. Use the answer options the checklist file lists.
- Each answer carries the paper's location and a short basis (section, page, table or figure). When the paper does not say, answer "Can't tell" (AMSTAR 2: No, with what is missing written down). Write "無法判斷" (cannot tell) in the report language where the file says Can't tell.
- **Not applicable.** When a question does not suit this study's design, answer "不適用：<reason>" (not applicable, with the reason in one sentence) instead of Can't tell. Not applicable means the question does not fit the design; Can't tell means the paper does not give the information. Do not use it to skip a question that fits but is hard to answer. AMSTAR 2 has its own "No meta-analysis conducted" option and does not use this.
  Questions that are often not applicable:
  - CASP systematic review, question 7 (precision): no statistical combination, only counts or descriptive tables.
  - CASP systematic review, question 10 (benefits worth the harms and costs): no intervention, for example an association or a descriptive review.
- Judge relative to the user's research question where a question asks about "your population" or "your context".
- Never add answers up into a score, a percentage or a grade. Do not turn a count of Yes into a verdict.
- Where a checklist asks for an appraisal summary or an overall confidence level, write it as that file describes.
- Copy only short pieces of a checklist into the report. Question numbers and a short restatement are enough; do not paste whole questions with their hints.
- CASP files are licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. If the report quotes or adapts CASP text, say it comes from CASP and under which licence. For MMAT and AMSTAR 2, cite the source named in the file.
