# AMSTAR 2: appraisal of systematic reviews of healthcare interventions

**Source.** Shea BJ, Reeves BC, Wells G, et al. AMSTAR 2: a critical appraisal tool for systematic reviews that include randomised or non-randomised studies of healthcare interventions, or both. BMJ 2017;358:j4008. doi:10.1136/bmj.j4008. © BMJ 2017, open access under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0): <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/>. The 16 questions and the two boxes below are taken from that paper's text. Changes made: the "How to use" section was added and the answer rules were written as a list. The official form with its detailed criteria for Yes and Partial Yes is the paper's supplementary appendix 1; this file does not reproduce it.

## How to use

Use this checklist for a systematic review (with or without meta-analysis) of healthcare interventions that includes randomised or non-randomised studies, or both.

- **Answers.** The "not applicable" and "cannot answer" options of the original AMSTAR were removed. The official form (the paper's supplementary figure, "the AMSTAR 2 instrument") sets these options per item, and this file does not reproduce its detailed criteria for Yes and Partial Yes; read the form for them.

  | Items | Answer options |
  |:---|:---|
  | 2, 4, 7, 8 | Yes, Partial Yes, No |
  | 9 | Yes, Partial Yes, No, and "Includes only NRSI" (when judging the RCT part) or "Includes only RCTs" (when judging the NRSI part): judge randomised and non-randomised studies separately |
  | 11 | Yes, No, "No meta-analysis conducted": judge RCTs and non-randomised studies separately |
  | 12, 15 | Yes, No, "No meta-analysis conducted" |
  | 1, 3, 5, 6, 10, 13, 14, 16 | Yes, No |

  Use "No meta-analysis conducted" when no meta-analysis was performed (for item 15, no quantitative synthesis).
- **Not enough information:** do not give the benefit of the doubt; answer No and write what is missing.
- For every item write the paper's location and the basis for the answer.
- **Never add the answers up into a score.** The authors strongly recommend that individual item ratings are not combined into an overall score.
- Finish with the overall confidence rating below and name every critical flaw and non-critical weakness that led to it.

## The 16 items

1. Did the research questions and inclusion criteria for the review include the components of PICO?
2. Did the report of the review contain an explicit statement that the review methods were established prior to the conduct of the review and did the report justify any significant deviations from the protocol? **(critical)**
3. Did the review authors explain their selection of the study designs for inclusion in the review?
4. Did the review authors use a comprehensive literature search strategy? **(critical)**
5. Did the review authors perform study selection in duplicate?
6. Did the review authors perform data extraction in duplicate?
7. Did the review authors provide a list of excluded studies and justify the exclusions? **(critical)**
8. Did the review authors describe the included studies in adequate detail?
9. Did the review authors use a satisfactory technique for assessing the risk of bias (RoB) in individual studies that were included in the review? **(critical)**
10. Did the review authors report on the sources of funding for the studies included in the review?
11. If meta-analysis was performed, did the review authors use appropriate methods for statistical combination of results? **(critical)**
12. If meta-analysis was performed, did the review authors assess the potential impact of RoB in individual studies on the results of the meta-analysis or other evidence synthesis?
13. Did the review authors account for RoB in primary studies when interpreting/discussing the results of the review? **(critical)**
14. Did the review authors provide a satisfactory explanation for, and discussion of, any heterogeneity observed in the results of the review?
15. If they performed quantitative synthesis did the review authors carry out an adequate investigation of publication bias (small study bias) and discuss its likely impact on the results of the review? **(critical)**
16. Did the review authors report any potential sources of conflict of interest, including any funding they received for conducting the review?

The seven critical items (2, 4, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15) are the authors' suggestion; appraisers may add or substitute other critical domains, and if you do, say so in the report. The authors' examples: leaving out non-randomised studies (item 3) in a review of adverse outcomes of treatment may be a critical flaw, and so may being unable to explain large variations in treatment effects across the studies (item 14). The rating scheme below is advisory.

## Rating the overall confidence in the results of the review

Neither the paper nor the form defines "critical flaw" and "non-critical weakness" item by item (the guidance document only says that flaws in critical domains greatly weaken confidence); this file reads them as follows (our reading, not the authors' wording): a critical flaw is a No on a critical item, and a non-critical weakness is a No on any other item. Partial Yes is not counted as a flaw. Items answered "No meta-analysis conducted" are not flaws. Say in the report when a Partial Yes or a substituted critical domain affected the rating.

| Overall confidence | Condition |
|:---|:---|
| High | No or one non-critical weakness: the review provides an accurate and comprehensive summary of the results of the available studies that address the question of interest |
| Moderate | More than one non-critical weakness, but no critical flaws |
| Low | One critical flaw, with or without non-critical weaknesses |
| Critically low | More than one critical flaw, with or without non-critical weaknesses |

Multiple non-critical weaknesses may diminish confidence in the review enough to move a rating from Moderate to Low; if you do this, say so and why.

Write the confidence level in the report as one of the four words above (translated into the report language if needed, with the English term in brackets), never as a number.
